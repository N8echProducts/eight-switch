# Eight Switch Toggle DIY edition build guide

## materials

Your Eight-Switch Toggle DIY edition should have the following in the package:
1. 1 circuit board
1. 1 button
1. 8 toggle switches
1. 8 resistors
1. 8 white LEDs
1. 1 Raspberry Pi Pico
1. 1 switch installation bracket (the 3d printed thing)

You will also need:
1. A soldering iron
1. A spool of rosin-core solder (lead free is preferred and recommended for your safety, but leaded works better)
1. Some soldering flux (technically optional, but will make the soldering work better)
1. A helping-hands tool to keep your hands free while soldering
1. Side-cutters (to cut off excess leads)

## Assembly

You can put the different parts in and install them however you want, however, the order I've provided will make for the easiest assembly.

1. Solder the Toggle Switches into the board
    1. Place the toggle switches into the installation bracket
    2. Put the installation bracket with the switches in place on the circuit board
    3. While holding the installation bracket firmly to the board, turn the board over to access the switch pins
    4. Use your hot soldering iron to heat up the toggle switch lead and the hole in the circuit board at the same time
    5. Slowly feed solder into the joint where the hole and the toggle switch lead meet.
2. Solder the resistors onto the circuit board
    1. The resistor holes are the ones just above each Switch.
    2. Place the resistor leads into the resistor holes.
    3. Bend the resistor leads down to hold the resistors in temporarily to work on the other side of the board.
    4. Use your hot soldering iron to heat up the resistor lead and the hole in the board at the same time.
    5. Slowly feed solder into the joint where the hole and the resistor lead meet.
3. Solder the LEDs onto the circuit board
    1. Place the leads of the led through the slot in the keyswitch, and through the holes in the circuit board

4. Solder the button into the circuit board

    2. turn over the board and solder the leads in
5. Solder the Raspberry Pi Pico onto the circuit board

6. Plug in your eight-switch Keyboard and try it out!
